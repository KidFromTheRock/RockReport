Get Seen

Exposure Is Not a Recruiting Plan

Getting seen matters. But exposure by itself is not a strategy.

A camp invite, a social media clip, a 7-on-7 weekend or a coach follow can all feel like movement. Sometimes they are. Sometimes they are just noise. The difference is whether the family has a plan behind the activity.

A real recruiting plan starts before the next event. It asks where the athlete fits, which schools actually need the position, whether the film tells the right story, how the academic profile lines up and what the family should say when college personnel start asking questions.

What families should organize first

  • Film: Put the best evaluation clips early. Make the position, size, speed, effort and football IQ easy to see.
  • Academics: Keep transcripts, GPA, test plans and eligibility timelines in the conversation from the start.
  • Target schools: Build a realistic list across levels, not just a wish list of logos.
  • Communication: Know who contacts coaches, what gets sent and how often follow-up happens.
  • Camp strategy: Choose events with a purpose: evaluation, exposure, relationship-building or development.

The short view: Exposure opens a door only when the athlete’s profile, film, academics and family communication are ready to support it.

For families, the goal is not to chase every event or every post. The goal is to be prepared enough that when the right person watches, the full picture is already clear.
