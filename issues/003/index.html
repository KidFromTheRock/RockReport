This Week

The New Football Path Has More Than One Playbook.

Recruiting is still about fit, film and development. But the modern path also includes agency education, NIL, revenue sharing and family financial readiness.

This week, we start with the Kid From The Rock lane — building a real recruiting plan — then bring in Red Envelope Sports on representation and The Seiler Group on the difference between revenue sharing and NIL.

The Rundown
  • Get Seen: why exposure is not the same thing as a recruiting plan.
  • Football Business: what an agency actually does for an athlete.
  • Money Game: revenue sharing vs. NIL, and why the difference matters.
  • From the Rock: Falelua Moalele’s path from American Samoa to Hawai‘i.
Get Seen

Exposure Is Not a Recruiting Plan

The short view:
A camp invite, a social media clip or a coach follow can feel like movement. But exposure only helps when the athlete’s film, academics, target list and family communication are ready to support it.

Why it matters: Families should not chase every event. They should build a clear plan that makes the right exposure count.

Money Game

Understanding Revenue Sharing and NIL

Student-athletes may now encounter both school revenue-sharing payments and third-party NIL income. The opportunity is real, but so are the planning questions.

The short view:
NIL and revenue sharing are related, but they are not the same. Families should understand where the money comes from before deciding what to do with it.
