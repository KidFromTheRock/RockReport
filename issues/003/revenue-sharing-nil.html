Money Game

Understanding School Revenue Sharing and Third-Party Marketing NIL

Money Game Presented By

College athletics is entering a new financial era. Families now need to understand two different conversations that can happen at the same time: school revenue sharing and third-party marketing NIL.

School revenue sharing allows Division I schools to directly compensate student-athletes using a portion of revenue generated by things such as media rights, ticket sales, sponsorships and other athletic department revenue streams.

Third-party marketing NIL — Name, Image and Likeness — is different. Third-party marketing NIL money generally comes from third-party endorsements, sponsorships, appearances, camps, social media and other personal branding opportunities.

That distinction matters because a student-athlete’s total compensation may include both school revenue sharing payments and third-party marketing NIL income. The money may be structured differently, reported differently and require different planning.

The bottom line

Athletes can receive both school revenue sharing and third-party marketing NIL payments at the same time. That creates opportunity, but it also creates responsibility.

Families should begin thinking about recordkeeping, tax planning, bank account structure, possible business entities and how each income source fits into the athlete’s broader financial picture.

The short view: Third-party marketing NIL and school revenue sharing are related, but they are not the same. Families should understand where the money comes from before deciding what to do with it.
